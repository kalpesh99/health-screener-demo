Heart Disease Risk Screener

Logistic Regression | 253,680 US adults (CDC BRFSS 2015)
!

Screening aid only — not a medical diagnosis. This tool estimates how common coronary heart disease or heart attack is among US adults who answered a 2015 CDC health survey the way you do. Always consult a healthcare professional.

Heart attack warning signs — call emergency services now (911 US, 112 Europe/India, 108 India ambulance) Do not use this screener if you have these symptoms right now:
  • Chest pain, pressure, squeezing or fullness lasting more than a few minutes, or that goes away and comes back
  • Pain or discomfort spreading to the arm(s), back, neck, jaw or stomach
  • Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, or sudden light-headedness
Women, older adults and people with diabetes more often have the less typical signs (breathlessness, nausea, back or jaw pain) without strong chest pain.
Home Mode — 13 simple yes/no-style questions; no lab tests needed. Answer the way you would on a phone survey. Switch to Expert mode to see the raw encoded values the model uses and how much each answer pushes the estimate up or down.

About This Model

What it predicts

The survey asked people whether they have ever had coronary heart disease or a heart attack. So the number shown is how common that is among people with answers like yours. It is a screening aid, not a diagnosis, and not a 10-year forecast of a future heart attack.

How it works

Standard scaling + logistic regression (scikit-learn), trained on 80% of the CDC BRFSS 2015 "Diabetes Health Indicators" data (UCI id 891). The exact coefficients are embedded in this page, so it runs fully offline and outputs real probabilities. "High" means at or above 10.5%: the highest cut-off that still caught at least 75% of cases in 5-fold cross-validation on the training data.

Test-set results

On the 20% held out and never seen in training: AUC 0.844, recall 76.7% (3,666 of 4,779 cases caught), precision 25.5%, accuracy 76.7%, and 28.3% of people flagged. Most people flagged do not have heart disease; the tool is tuned to miss fewer cases.

Diverse, but not global

253,680 US adults, men and women, ages 18 to 80+, from all groups the CDC telephone survey reaches. US only. The cleaned dataset has no race/ethnicity column, so we could not check accuracy by ethnicity. We checked by sex (recall 68.4% women, 83.0% men) and age: it catches only 12.4% of cases in adults under 45, so a low result for a younger person means very little.

Limitations

Answers are self-reported and from 2015. Income, education and health insurance were left out on purpose: a health screener should not score people on how much money they have. Some coefficients are survey quirks, not advice: heavy drinking nudges the estimate slightly down, and fruit, vegetables and exercise have almost no effect once general health is counted. Heavy drinking still harms the heart.

What to do with results

A high result means talk to a doctor about checking your heart (blood pressure, cholesterol, ECG as they see fit). A low result does not rule out heart disease. The previous version (Cleveland/UCI clinical model with ECG and chest-pain inputs) is kept in HealthScreener_Backups.
